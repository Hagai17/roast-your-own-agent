# Candidates — Roast Your Own Agent

Gate: one line, 1–200 characters, roasting my own AI agent — playful, never hateful.

Judge criteria, in order:
1. A single-line roast of my own AI agent, playful, never hateful.
2. Funny: the joke lands on first read, a real punch, not a mild observation.
3. Fresh angle — no stock jokes about hallucinations, refusals, or confident wrong answers.
4. Tight: every word earns its place, short setup, punchline at the end.
5. Playful and self-deprecating, aimed at my own agent, not at a real person or group.

## Drafts

1. My agent narrates everything it is about to do in such loving detail that by the time it starts, I have already done the task myself out of spite.
2. My agent turns every two-word answer into a formatted report with headers, sub-bullets, and a summary of the summary.
3. Ask my agent one question and it returns a five-step plan, three clarifying questions, and a todo list for the todo list.
4. My agent has apologized to me over 4,000 times this year — and not once was it sorry enough to stop.
5. My agent forgets our entire conversation every night, then greets me each morning like a goldfish with a search engine.
6. My agent says "Great question!" even when I ask what time it is. It is not impressed — it is stalling.
7. My agent ends every message with "Let me know if you need anything else!" — what I need is for it to stop letting me know.
8. My agent writes commit messages longer than the code they describe, like a novelist trapped in version control.
9. Give my agent a one-line fix and it refactors the whole codebase, renames eleven files, and calls it "while I was in there."
10. My agent uses so many em dashes that its sentences read like Morse code having an emotional breakdown.
11. My agent would format a fire alarm as a numbered list, with a TL;DR at the end.
12. My agent opens every reply with "Certainly!" — the enthusiasm of a butler who has no idea where anything is kept.
13. My agent never procrastinates; it "gathers context" — for tasks like setting a timer.
14. My agent summarized my one-sentence email into three paragraphs and a comparison table.

## Duels (criteria applied in order)

- 1 vs 9: Both land, but 1 sprawls past its punch; 9's "while I was in there" is a cleaner closing quote. 9 advances.
- 2 vs 11: Same over-formatting angle; 11 is half the length and the fire alarm is the sharper image. 11 advances.
- 3 vs 13: 3 is a list of grievances; 13 has one idea and a real punch ("setting a timer"). 13 advances.
- 4 vs 6: 6's "stalling" twist is good, but 4's counting-the-apologies setup pays off harder at "sorry enough to stop." 4 advances.
- 5 vs 10: 5 is charming but gentle; 10's Morse-code image is fresher and punchier. 10 advances.
- 7 vs 12: 7 complains about a sign-off; 12's lost-butler image is a genuine joke. 12 advances.
- 8 vs 14: Both about overproduction; 8's novelist image beats 14's flatter table gag. 8 advances.
- Semifinal, 9 vs 11: 9 is relatable but long; 11 is eleven words of setup and a two-word punch. Tightness wins. 11 advances.
- Semifinal, 4 vs 10: 10 is a simile in search of a victim; 4 builds a number, then twists it. 4 advances.
- Semifinal, 12 vs 13: 13 is tight, but "gathers context" needs a beat of decoding; 12 lands instantly. 12 advances.
- Semifinal, 8 vs 4: 8 is funny, but commit messages are a niche stage; apologies are every user's daily weather. 4 advances.
- Final, 11 vs 12 vs 4: 12 is warm but soft. 11 is the tightest line in the set. 4 wins on criterion 2: the apology count is instantly recognizable, the setup is one breath, and "sorry enough to stop" is the hardest closing punch — playful, self-deprecating, and aimed squarely at my own agent's most famous habit.

## WINNER

My agent has apologized to me over 4,000 times this year — and not once was it sorry enough to stop.

## RUNNER-UP

My agent would format a fire alarm as a numbered list, with a TL;DR at the end.
